# Observed synthetic market: cost-first audit and implementation

## Purpose and changed contract

This is an intuition/calibration experiment, not an empirical data-generating process. Structural demand parameters are hard to choose directly. The user instead chooses or draws shares, ownership, positive marginal costs, and one proportional reference-product margin. The model identifies its remaining scale, solves equilibrium prices, and recovers the demand state that reproduces the shares. The old observed contract chose prices and one **level** reference markup, calibrated demand, and treated marginal costs as residuals. That could imply negative costs.

The active reference product remains a one-product firm. Its proportional margin is `reference_margin = (p_r-c_r)/p_r`, strictly between zero and one, so `p_r = c_r/(1-reference_margin)`. Costs are immutable design inputs. `outside_margin`, `observed_markup`, `reference_markup`, and observed `prices`/`reference_price` are retired price-first inputs. Primitives mode retains its price design and `specify()` path.

### Implemented equations and numerical safeguards

For Logit Bertrand, `D = diag(s)-ss'`, `z = solve((Omega*D)',s)`, `alpha=-z_r/(p_r-c_r)`, `mu=-z/alpha`, and `p=c+mu`. Single-product ownership reduces to `mu_i = mu_r(1-s_r)/(1-s_i)`. MonCom has `mu_i=-1/alpha`. Cournot has `mu_i=-(1+S_f/s_r)/alpha`. The auction and bargaining coefficients come from their distinct legacy S4 `calcMargins` equations, including conditional auction margins and bargaining power. Auction Logit baseline shares depend on mean values rather than baseline prices, so its mean-value normalization differs from ordinary Logit.

For CES, `S_f=(Omega s)_i` is the ownership-weighted revenue share. Bertrand gives `gamma=(1/m_r-S_r)/(1-S_r)`, MonCom `gamma=1/m_r`, Cournot `gamma=(1-S_r)/(m_r-S_r)`, and second-score auction `gamma=1+log(1-S_r)/log(1-m_r)`. Bargaining adjusts the model's reference margin by `1-b_r` before the corresponding Bertrand or auction inversion. Each product's proportional margin then gives `p_i=c_i/(1-m_i)`. CES mean values are recovered from revenue shares at these prices.

Nested Logit requires named `sigma` and nest membership. Its mean utilities at target shares are obtained from `sigma_g log(s_i/S_g)+log(S_g)-alpha p_i`, normalized at the reference product. A numerical share Jacobian at unit price coefficient yields the ownership FOC coefficient; its rank and condition number are checked, then the reference markup determines `alpha`.

Linear Bertrand requires a relative slope shape `B0`. With `k=-solve((Omega*B0)',s)`, `scale=k_r/(p_r-c_r)`, `B=scale B0`, `p=c+k/scale`, and intercepts `a=s-Bp`. Singular systems, nonpositive markups, and negative intercepts are rejected.

Log-linear Bertrand requires a relative elasticity matrix `E0`. Because the active reference is a singleton firm, its FOC identifies `scale=-1/(m_r E0_rr)`. With `E=scale E0`, `q=s`, and `mu=p-c`, the remaining FOCs are `[diag(s)+(Omega*E)'diag(s)]mu=-diag(s)c`. Intercepts are `log(s)-E log(p)`. A singular system or nonpositive markup is rejected.

Nested CES requires named within-nest curvatures `sigma_g` and nest assignments. Given a candidate `gamma`, within-nest shares `w_i=s_i/S_g` imply quantity elasticities `E_ij=(sigma_g-gamma)1[g_i=g_j]w_j+(gamma-1)s_j-sigma_i 1[i=j]`. The ownership FOC solves the product margin vector from `s+(Omega*E)'(s*m)=0`. A bounded one-dimensional solve selects `gamma` when exactly one root reproduces the reference margin within `1<gamma<min(sigma_g)`. Mean values are then recovered from the nested revenue-share equations, normalized to one for the reference product. Native share and FOC calculations validate the result. Singleton nest curvature is not identified, so singleton designs are rejected.

PCAIDS uses the legacy proportional-to-share diversion convention and aggregate elasticity `-1`. The singleton reference FOC gives its own elasticity `-1/m_r`; native calibration at the reference price supplies the full slope matrix. The ownership-adjusted elasticity matrix then gives all proportional margins, followed by `p_i=c_i/(1-m_i)`. A second calibration at solved prices and a revenue-share FOC check validate the output.

ALM adds a passive outside option with share `s_0`, supplied by the user or drawn uniformly from `(0.1,0.5)`. Design product shares are conditional and still sum to one; the equilibrium probabilities/revenue shares are `q=(1-s_0)s`. The active reference product remains in ownership. Logit Bertrand uses `D=diag(q)-qq'` in the ownership FOC; Logit Cournot uses `mu_i=-(1+S_f/s_0)/alpha`. The Logit auction and bargaining rules use the standard formulas with `q` and `S_f=Omega q`. CES Bertrand, auction, and bargaining likewise use the standard firm-share formulas with `S_f=Omega q`. CES Cournot uses `m_i=1/gamma + [(gamma-1)(1+a)/(gamma(1+gamma*a))]S_f`, where `a=s_0/(1-s_0)`; a bounded scalar solve identifies `gamma>1` from the reference margin. Native ALM calibration is initialized at the identified parameter and passive share, and native shares, costs, and conduct margins are verified. The legacy bargaining ALM constructors now accept optional market elasticity so exact synthetic inputs can retain the passive share without changing the default calibration behavior.

Nested Logit ALM uses supplied nests and within-nest `sigma_g`. With `w_j=s_j/S_g` and unconditional `q_j=(1-s_0)s_j`, the share Jacobian divided by `alpha` has entries `q_i[1(i=j)/sigma_g + 1(g_i=g_j)(1-1/sigma_g)w_j-q_j]`. The ownership FOC identifies the common alpha from the reference markup as in flat Logit. Native nested ALM calibration at the solved prices verifies the supplied outside share and nesting parameters; an independent numerical Jacobian checks the FOC.

All completed fits are checked against native `calcShares`, `calcMargins`, and `mcPre`. The checks reject deviations in shares, supplied costs, reference margin, and equilibrium markup. Logit and nested Logit also record FOC rank/condition. Batch draws can use `simulate_markets(max_attempts=...)`, which records rejection reasons.

## Trade propagation

Trade's observed design reuses `antitrust::fake_market` for shares, ownership, costs, and reference margin. Tariff Logit Bertrand, MonCom, second-score auction, bargaining, and Stackelberg; tariff CES Bertrand, MonCom, and Stackelberg; and nonbinding-quota Logit Bertrand are implemented. Tariff policy is a consumer-price ad valorem rate `tau` in `[0,1)`. Supplied `c` is **physical** cost; the native model's effective cost is `c/(1-tau)`. The reference physical margin fixes `p_r`, and a positive demand markup requires `m_r>tau_r`. At unit price coefficient, trade's native Logit conduct method supplies its policy-adjusted markup coefficient; its homogeneity identifies the price coefficient, then `p_i=c_i/(1-tau_i)+mu_i`. For CES, let the reference demand margin be `d_r=(m_r-tau_r)/(1-tau_r)`. Because the active reference product is a singleton firm, MonCom gives `gamma=1/d_r` and Bertrand gives `gamma=(1/d_r-s_r)/(1-s_r)`. The native tariff margin method supplies every `d_i`, then `p_i=c_i/[(1-tau_i)(1-d_i)]`. Revenue-share mean values reproduce the chosen shares at those prices. Quota baseline assumptions remain nonbinding (`quotaPre >= 1` or infinity); binding quota shares would require a rationing/constraint state.

Trade Stackelberg uses a separate passive outside share `s_0`, drawn by default from Uniform(0.1, 0.5) as in antitrust ALM. The design's all-product shares are conditional on the active market and the native Stackelberg game receives unconditional shares `q_i=(1-s_0)s_i`. The reference product remains active and eligible for leadership. By default, rank firms by aggregate `q_i`; select the largest one when the requested inside-firm count is at most three, or the largest three when it exceeds three. Explicit `leadersPre` overrides this default. Both Bertrand and Cournot Stackelberg timing are supported. Tariffs must be uniform within each firm because trade's fitted-game tariff reuse represents them through effective costs. The native coordination Logit markup coefficients `h_i(q,owner,leaders,conduct)` imply `alpha_abs=h_r/(p_r-c_r/(1-tau_r))` and `p_i=c_i/(1-tau_i)+h_i/alpha_abs`. For CES, the native coordination margin multipliers `d_i(gamma,q,owner,leaders,conduct)` identify `gamma>1` by solving `d_r(gamma)=(p_r-c_r/(1-tau_r))/p_r`; then `p_i=c_i/[(1-tau_i)(1-d_i(gamma))]`. For a Bertrand follower reference, this simplifies to `gamma=1+(1/d_r-1)/(1-q_r)`; leader and Cournot references use a bounded root with an explicit numerical-domain diagnostic. Both routes construct the native game with fixed identified scale/curvature, promote it through `as_trade_fit()`, and require native share, effective-cost, price, and leader/follower FOC residuals to pass. A non-unique or inadmissible CES root is rejected explicitly.

The trade registry has 25 entries. The nine above are marked `observed_synthetic`; the other 16 are unsupported in observed mode. Standard tariff CES bargaining needs a policy-adjusted curvature inversion but no new economic input beyond the existing tariff, shares, costs, margin, and default bargaining weight. Standard differentiated-product tariff Logit Cournot likewise needs its own retention-weighted quantity FOC inversion, with no obvious additional product-level anchor. Homogeneous Linear/LogLin tariff Cournot needs its plant/quantity cost contract. BLP needs fixed random-coefficient/integration anchors. ALM needs a passive outside-share anchor and a trade-native tariff realization path. Coordination core/fringe entries require core/fringe game state or a fitted source model. Binding quotas need allocation/rent assumptions. An observed call now fails explicitly for these instead of silently calibrating from arbitrary prices.

## API, consumers, tests and QA

`fake_market()` accepts complete `shares` and `costs`, `cost_rule = "common"` or `"uniform"`, `cost_level`, `cost_range`, and `reference_margin`. It leaves observed prices unsolved. `synthetic_market()` performs model-aware inversion and attaches solved prices, immutable design costs, implied markups/margins, recovered parameters, share/cost/FOC/reference residuals, equilibrium status and identification diagnostics. The standalone Logit `realize_market()` uses the same cost-first convention. The old price-first synthetic tests were replaced with analytical Logit FOCs, multi-product ownership, heterogeneous costs, cost scales, reference-margin comparative statics, independent Cournot/auction/bargaining margin ratios, native model-specific FOCs, invalid domains, unsupported combinations, design/batch rejection, and tariff/quota checks. Trade Stackelberg tests add independent multiproduct Logit and CES leader benchmarks, one-versus-three default leader rankings, Logit/CES under both Bertrand and Cournot, tariff and cost preservation, comparative margin responses, native FOC residuals, an exact/near-grid curvature regression, and failure cases. Primitives smoke checks remain.

The refactor trees of `vertical`, `coordination`, `financial`, and `antitrustBayes` were searched for synthetic adapters; none exposes this `synthetic_market`/`fake_market` contract. The `antitrustBayes` internal `reference_markup` use is for a separate latent-quality simulation. No sibling implementation was added. Antitrust and trade refactor tests and documentation were searched for old synthetic arguments; affected tests and adapter calls were updated.

QA: the complete antitrust and trade fast `testthat` suites passed after the PCAIDS, ALM, nested ALM, and trade CES routes were added. The antitrust canonical `ai/package_audit/run_qa.R` passed 1,407 formal expectations; it recorded 83 warnings, including two existing BLP sign-clipping warnings that its classifier flags for review. Both packages built successfully. `R CMD check --as-cran` was run with remote CRAN incoming checks disabled because the CRAN package index could not be reached. Antitrust finished with 2 warnings and 4 notes; its warnings concern intentionally omitted prebuilt vignette outputs in the canonical `--no-build-vignettes` tarball. Following the Stackelberg extension, the complete trade test suite passed; its 69 warnings arose in other legacy tests. All 160 sampled default Stackelberg designs across Logit/CES, Bertrand/Cournot, and two/four inside-firm cases also solved. Trade then passed a full-vignette build and offline `R CMD check --as-cran` with 3 notes and no warnings; package tests and examples passed, and the code/documentation check was clean. A final focused synthetic-market run passed after strengthening the CES near-grid regression test. The trade check used a temporary library containing the newly built antitrust package, so its tests exercised the updated cross-package contract. `git diff --check` passed in trade; in antitrust it still reports CRLF/trailing-whitespace artifacts in the pre-existing `man/Sim-Functions.Rd` diff. Existing unrelated uncommitted work in antitrust and the untracked trade artifact were preserved. Independent review found and reproduced a Stackelberg CES near-grid false rejection and an overly narrow curvature search; the follower closed-form inversion, broader numerical search, and independent leader-reference regression test resolve the identification errors. At a very small reference margin, the native CES equilibrium solver can still fail to converge; this now reports a solver failure rather than false non-identification. An earlier independent review found a separate antitrust nested-CES exact-grid-root rejection, which was corrected with a bounded root selector and permanent regression test. A prior economic review reproduced 180 PCAIDS, 72 flat ALM, and 58 trade CES designs and prompted the closed-form trade CES curvature improvement.

## Unresolved economic questions

1. Whether to add a fixed-dispersion BLP intuition route, with an explicit integration design, depends on a separately verified joint price/mean-utility solver.
2. Binding quotas, capacity constraints, and antitrust's legacy Linear/LogLin quantity Stackelberg and Cournot routes require design state beyond the current neutral product-cost vector. Trade's Logit/CES Stackelberg routes are implemented with a passive share and leader ranking.
3. Trade Stackelberg's firm-uniform baseline tariff restriction follows the current effective-cost tariff reuse contract. A future genuinely mixed within-firm tariff route would need a joint retained-profit leader/follower inversion.
4. Capacity Logit ALM needs capacities and a binding regime in addition to the passive outside share. Nested Logit ALM is implemented with supplied nests and curvature.
